Privacy policy

Effective November 22, 2026

This policy explains what information is collected when you visit rizobot.com (the "site"), and how the Rizo extension for VS Code and the Rizo Lang compiler handle your data. The site and both projects are run by Chaitanya Aggarwal, an individual developer in the United States ("we", "us").

The short version: the site counts visits with Google Analytics only if you accept. The Rizo extension has no telemetry and no Rizo server: your code goes straight from your computer to the AI provider you choose. Rizo Lang runs entirely on your machine and servers. We don't sell personal information or use it for advertising.

1. The website

What we collect

  • Server logs. Our host, Vercel, processes technical data needed to deliver pages and protect the site, such as your IP address, browser type, the page requested and the time. See Vercel's privacy policy.
  • Analytics, only with your consent. If you choose "Accept" in the cookie notice, we load Google Analytics 4. It sets first-party cookies (named _ga and _ga_<id>) and collects pages viewed, how you arrived, approximate location (city or region, derived from your IP address, which Google Analytics 4 does not store), device and browser type, and clicks on install, download and GitHub links.

The site has no forms, accounts, ads or comments, and does not ask for your name or email address. If you email us, we receive what you send.

How we use it

To keep the site running and secure, and to understand in aggregate which pages are useful. We have turned off Google signals, ad personalization and advertising features, and we don't link analytics to any advertising product.

Your choices

  • Consent. Analytics stays off until you accept. You can change your choice at any time with "Cookie settings" at the bottom of every page.
  • Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, we treat it as a refusal: analytics never loads and we don't ask.
  • Google's opt-out. You can also install the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on.
  • Cross-site tracking. We don't allow other parties to track you across websites through this site.

Who receives it

Vercel (hosting) and, only with your consent, Google (analytics), each as a service provider processing data for us. Information may be processed in the United States and other countries where they operate. Learn more in how Google uses information from sites that use its services.

How long we keep it

Google Analytics data is kept for 2 months, the shortest period it offers, and then deleted. Server logs are kept by Vercel according to its own retention periods.

2. The Rizo extension for VS Code

  • No telemetry, no Rizo server. The extension doesn't send usage data to us, and we never receive your code, prompts or keys.
  • Your AI provider. When you send a message, the extension sends your prompt and the code and file contents needed for the task directly from your computer to the provider you chose: OpenRouter (which forwards it to the model's company), your Azure AI Foundry resource, or Amazon Bedrock. That provider's terms and privacy policy govern what happens next, including retention and whether data may be used for training. Review them, and your account settings there, before sending sensitive code.
  • Other connections. The extension also reads OpenRouter's public model list, and connects to MCP servers only if you add them yourself.
  • Stored on your computer. Chats, settings and undo snapshots are stored by VS Code on your computer. API keys are kept in your operating system's keychain through VS Code.
  • Secrets. Files that usually hold secrets, such as .env and private keys, are only read with your approval and are never searched or sent for autocomplete. Secrets found in command output are redacted before they're sent.

The VS Code Marketplace and GitHub are run by Microsoft and GitHub under their own privacy policies.

3. Rizo Lang

Rizo Lang is a compiler that runs on your computer. It doesn't send data to us. Apps you build with it run on infrastructure you choose, and you are responsible for how those apps handle their users' data. Downloading the package from npm is subject to npm's privacy policy.

4. Children

The site and our software are made for software developers. They are not directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we'll delete it.

5. Your rights

Depending on where you live, including the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and some US states, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or export personal information about you, to object to or restrict its use, and to withdraw consent at any time. Because we collect very little and don't tie analytics to your identity, we may not be able to find records about you, but we'll help where we can. Write to us at the address below. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

For visitors in the EEA and UK, we rely on your consent for analytics and on our legitimate interest in running a secure site for server logs.

We do not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising, as those terms are defined under California law.

6. Security

The site is served only over HTTPS with a strict Content Security Policy and no third-party scripts other than Google Analytics after consent. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, but we keep what we collect to a minimum.

7. Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update it here and change the effective date at the top. If a change is significant, we'll also note it on the home page.
